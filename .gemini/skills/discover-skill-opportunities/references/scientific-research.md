# Scientific research opportunity patterns

## High-value recurring clusters

| Cluster | Evidence signals | Likely reusable contents | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Literature search and evidence synthesis | Repeated queries, screening, extraction, citation cleanup | query protocol, inclusion rubric, extraction schema | search is reproducible and citations resolve |
| Experiment or analysis planning | Repeated design choices, controls, power or feasibility checks | protocol template, decision tree, preregistration checklist | hypotheses, endpoints and controls are explicit |
| Data preprocessing and QC | Similar cleaning, exclusion, normalization, plots | scripts, schemas, QC thresholds | provenance and QC reports are reproducible |
| Statistical analysis and sensitivity checks | Reused models and diagnostics | analysis scripts, assumption checklist | code, environment and outputs reproduce |
| Figure-to-narrative synthesis | Repeated panel interpretation and claim calibration | figure rubric, claim-evidence matrix | claims match data and uncertainty |
| Manuscript, grant, or submission compliance | Recurring formatting and completeness checks | journal/funder references, validators, checklists | required sections and files are present |

## Good skill boundaries

Package reproducible procedures, domain schemas, and validation—not conclusions. Split wet-lab, computational, clinical, and qualitative workflows when their controls differ. Keep project data and unpublished findings outside reusable references unless explicitly intended.

## Candidate evidence

Look for duplicated notebooks, repeated database queries, manual citation repair, recurring reviewer comments, inconsistent preprocessing, or repeated explanation of instruments and datasets.

## Risk controls

Preserve provenance, versions, units, exclusions, negative results, and uncertainty. Never fabricate citations or results. Require expert review for clinical, biosafety, ethics, authorship, and regulatory decisions.
