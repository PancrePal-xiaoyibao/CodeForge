# Legal and compliance opportunity patterns

## High-value recurring clusters

| Cluster | Evidence signals | Likely reusable contents | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract intake and clause review | Repeated clause comparisons, fallback positions, missing terms | clause library, issue checklist, playbook | deviations and unresolved issues listed |
| Policy mapping and control evidence | Recurring requirement-to-control mapping | obligation schema, control matrix, evidence tracker | every applicable obligation has status and owner |
| Privacy and data review | Repeated data-flow, retention, access, vendor questions | data inventory, review questionnaire, risk rubric | data categories and transfers accounted for |
| Regulatory monitoring | Recurring source checks and impact memos | jurisdiction map, source hierarchy, change log | current effective date and applicability confirmed |
| Incident or investigation chronology | Repeated evidence collection and timeline reconstruction | evidence register, chronology template, privilege labels | sources, custody and uncertainties recorded |
| Filing and approval readiness | Recurring completeness and sign-off checks | submission checklist, approval matrix | required materials and approvals present |

## Good skill boundaries

Package intake, issue spotting, evidence organization, and review preparation. Do not represent the skill as a lawyer, final legal opinion, regulator, or autonomous compliance decision maker. Keep jurisdiction- and organization-specific rules in dated references.

## Candidate evidence

Look for repeated redlines, copied questionnaires, recurring missing approvals, manual obligation tracking, or inconsistent retention and vendor reviews.

## Risk controls

Identify jurisdiction, effective date, privilege, confidentiality, authority, and source hierarchy. Require qualified counsel or compliance owner for interpretations, submissions, waivers, investigations, employment actions, or binding commitments.
